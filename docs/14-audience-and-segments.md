# 14 · Audience & Segments (18–60)

> **Decision:** Rustle is for adults **18–60**, in **English and French**. This document analyses the age bands inside that range and recommends where to focus the product and marketing.

---

## 1. The key insight: segment by *life moment*, not by age

A 22-year-old after a first big heartbreak and a 48-year-old after a divorce need the same thing from Rustle: to feel heard, remembered, and not judged. **The product is the same for everyone.** What changes by age is:
- **which life moments** are most common,
- **where people are reachable** (TikTok vs Facebook),
- **how they like to be spoken to** (tone, formality, emoji),
- **how much they trust AI** and **how willing they are to pay**,
- **design needs** (text size, simplicity).

So Rustle has **one product with adaptive tone**, and **several marketing entry doors** built around life moments.

| Quebec residents who have used a digital mental-health tool | ~**40%** | La Presse, June 2025 |

**What this means**
1. **Under-30s already use AI for emotional support**, so they're easier to acquire but skeptical and quick to churn. They need Rustle to feel *different* from ChatGPT (it's specific, it comes to you, it isn't a chat).
2. **30–60s use AI less for emotions**, which means **less competition from ChatGPT habits** and more room for a product that feels human and safe. They need trust signals (privacy, "not a chatbot", a human-reviewed safety approach).
3. **France has a big gap between distress and professional help** (55% vs 28%) and a strong cultural reluctance to talk about it, so a private, gentle, non-clinical tool fits. French users are privacy-conscious (86% worried), so **privacy must be a headline, not a footnote**, in French marketing.

## 4. Recommendation: where to focus

### Priority 1: **25–44, going through a breakup/divorce, burnout or job loss** 💰 (core paying users)
- These users have the highest willingness to pay, long-lasting life situations (so good retention), and are still reachable on Instagram, TikTok and podcasts.
- Messaging: *"For the nights when it's heavy and you don't want to explain it again."* / *« Pour les soirs où c'est lourd et où tu n'as pas envie de tout réexpliquer. »*
- **Revenue engine.**

### Priority 2: **18–24, exams and first heartbreak** 📣 (growth engine)
- This group is the most viral: they share cards, send warm notes to friends and make TikToks. They pay less, so use a student discount.
- Use exam seasons as launch moments: France (partiels in January and May, the bac in June), Quebec (cégep/university finals in December and April), and US/UK finals (April–June).
- **Awareness and viral engine.**

### Priority 3: **45–60, divorce, caregiving, illness, grief** 🌱 (second wave, month 3+)
- High lifetime value and loyalty, but slower and more expensive to acquire (Meta ads, Facebook groups).
- Extra entry door: **gifting and "send a warm note to your mum or dad"** from younger users.
- The product must be ready for them: large text, a simple mode, a "vous" option in French, and no slang by default.

### Gender
The affirmation category is mostly marketed to women, and early users will likely skew female. **Build for everyone, lead creative with women 25–44**, and **test separate creatives for men**, who are underserved and often more reluctant to seek support ("divorced dads", "after the layoff", "no one asks how *you're* doing"). Measure it with your own data rather than assuming.

## 5. What this changes in the product

| Area | Change |
|---|---|
| Onboarding | **Don't ask for age bands in the UI** beyond the 18+ gate. Tone comes from step 6 (tone preferences). Add a French-only "tu / vous" question. |
| AI tone | Tone preferences + recent reactions drive the voice. The golden eval set includes personas across 18–60 (student, 30s burnout, 40s divorce, 58-year-old caregiver). |
| Design | Dynamic Type, **Simple mode**, high-contrast theme and dictation are in the MVP scope (important for 45–60). |
| Paywall | Student discount (18–24); **lifetime option and gifting** tested for 45+. |
| Warm notes | "Send one to your mum/dad/friend" to reach older users. |
| Marketing | Separate creatives per life moment, not per age: "exams", "heartbreak", "divorce", "burnout/layoff", "caring for a parent". |

## 6. English vs French: market notes

| | English (US, UK, IE, CA, AU) | French (France, Quebec, Belgium, Switzerland) |
|---|---|---|
| Market | Largest subscription revenue pool; most competitors are here | The affirmation category in French looks **underdeveloped**: mostly translated apps ("I am – Pensée positive", small affirmation apps) and no strong personal/AI player found |
| Local competitors | I am, Finch, Rosebud, Calm, Headspace… | **Petit BamBou** (meditation, 12M+ users, €6.99/mo), **MindDay** (CBT/wellbeing, €13.99/mo · €79.99/yr), **Mon Sherpa** (AI/CBT chatbot, now free), Alan Mind (insurer), AI "psy" chat tools (Mon Psy IA and others) |
| Pricing norms | $7.99/mo · $39.99–69.99/yr | **€6–14/month, €40–80/year** → €7.99 / €44.99 fits well |
| Culture | "Self-care" and affirmation language is familiar | Affirmations can feel *"too American"* or *"guimauve"* (sappy) to French users, so lead with **"des mots justes / quelqu'un qui se souvient"** (the right words, someone who remembers) rather than "positive affirmations". Privacy concern is high, so lead with it. |
| Register | — | "tu" by default (warm, like a friend), with a "vous" option |
| Quebec | 9-8-8 crisis line, English + French | Quebec French vocabulary differs from France (e.g. *courriel*, *magasiner*, *chum/blonde*). Let the model match the user's own words, and have a Quebec reviewer check it. |
| Soft launch | **Canada** is ideal: English and French in one market, smaller and cheaper to test, with Quebec a strong French-speaking audience | Then France + Belgium + Switzerland |

## 7. Research to run (in discovery, weeks 1–3)

- 15–20 interviews: 4 per age band, a mix of EN and FR, men and women.
- Concierge test: 20–30 people, at least 8 French speakers and at least 6 aged 45+.
- Landing page test: an EN and an FR headline per life moment, to see which door converts best.
